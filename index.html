Why slots go empty

Late cancellations, no-shows, and patients who arrive unprepared all cost the department money, and some cost much more than others.

A missed X-ray costs a short time slot and a phone call to rebook.

A missed PET scan can waste the tracer dose made just for that patient. The expensive scanner sits empty, the technologist is left idle, and it's almost impossible to fill the slot on such short notice.

Where the breakdown begins

This is not an Epic problem.

Epic can book the appointment. It cannot make a rushed scheduler remember every prep rule, or make a worried patient remember a phone script.

Schedulers handle high call volume. Critical details get skipped. Patients then face medical language they do not understand. Some cancel because they are nervous. Some do not come. Some arrive and cannot be scanned.

Move the instructions into the patient’s hands.

In nuclear medicine, that pattern is familiar. A patient calls to cancel because the scan sounds frightening. A tech who explains the exam, the wait, and the prep often stops that cancel. The video gives that explanation the same way every time, before the patient decides to drop the appointment. It does not replace the tech. It handles the explanation the phone call keeps repeating.

A video will not stop a cancel caused by insurance or a changed order. It can reach the patient who is about to drop the exam because they do not understand it.

What the facility gets

The full outpatient list is covered.

Schedulers stay on booking

Schedulers do not have to deliver complex protocols on the call. Every patient gets the video for their exact exam and modality. When the video carries the prep, the scheduling call can stay on booking the exam.

Shorter calls, fewer prep mistakes

Calls get shorter, and prep mistakes from script fatigue drop. Shorter calls may let the same staff book more patients per day, and may shorten the wait to get that call.

More patients who show up.

The missed scan rate will not fall to zero. No one will expect that. Fewer fear-based cancels, fewer unprepared arrivals, and less prep work at the scheduling desk mean more booked scans get done.
